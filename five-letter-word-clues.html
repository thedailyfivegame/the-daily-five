How Five-Letter Word Puzzle Clues Work

The Daily Five · Published 18 November 2026

A good guess gives you information even when it is wrong. Here is how to read green, yellow and grey clues, handle repeated letters and choose your next five-letter word.

Play the daily word puzzle →

What the three tile colours mean

  • Green: the letter is correct and already in the right position.
  • Yellow: the word contains that letter, but you need to move it to another position.
  • Grey: that copy of the letter does not match an unused copy in the answer. Usually the letter is absent; repeated letters need a closer look.

Colour-blind mode uses a different palette. The meaning stays the same: correct position, present elsewhere, or absent.

A worked example: PLATE against PLANT

This is an illustrative practice example, not today’s answer. Imagine the hidden word is PLANT and you enter PLATE.

You now have PLA__, with T somewhere other than the fourth slot. PLANT fits every clue; PLATE does not. Checking a candidate against all earlier rows prevents wasted guesses.

Why the same letter can be yellow and grey

Repeated letters are counted. A coloured A tells you there is at least one A, not that every A you type must receive a colour. Correct-position matches are assigned first; the remaining letters use up the remaining copies.

For another practice example, use APPLE as the answer and ALLEY as the guess:

ALLEY

The first L is yellow because APPLE has an L elsewhere. The second L is grey because the answer has only one L. E is yellow because it belongs in the last position, not the fourth. Do not cross L out completely: the yellow tile still matters.

Choose a starting word that teaches you something

A familiar word with several different common letters, such as CRANE or STARE, gives you a useful opening spread. These are examples, not guaranteed best starters. Five distinct letters test more different possibilities than a word with repeated letters.

After the first row, follow the evidence. Keep confirmed positions, move yellow letters and try useful new letters. A fresh vowel may help, but a word that tests only vowels can leave you little information about the consonants.

A simple checklist before pressing Enter

  1. Does the word fit the green positions?
  2. Does it include the yellow letters in different positions?
  3. Have you avoided absent letters and respected repeated-letter counts?
  4. Will this guess help distinguish the candidates you still have?
