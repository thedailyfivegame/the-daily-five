Word Scramble Solving Tips

The Daily Five · Published 18 November 2026

Five mixed-up letters can look impossible until you change how you read them. Use vowel positions, familiar letter pairs and a quick letter-count check to find a candidate before you submit.

Play the daily word scramble →

Understand what you are solving

In The Daily Scramble, you rearrange five tiles into the selected five-letter word. Use every tile exactly once. The timer starts when you choose your first tile, and you get one submitted answer for the day.

You can rearrange the tiles before submitting. A familiar-looking word is worth checking: the same letters sometimes form several English words, while this game checks against one selected answer.

1. Separate vowels and consonants

Look for A, E, I, O and U first. Y can sometimes act as a vowel. Notice how many vowel tiles you have, then try likely positions around the consonants. This narrows the combinations you need to consider without assuming that every English word follows the same pattern.

For example, consider these practice letters, which are not today’s puzzle:

TRACE

A and E are your vowels. Try placing a consonant pair before A, or placing E at the end. You are looking for a recognisable structure, not testing all 120 possible arrangements.

2. Look for pairs that often stay together

Try combinations such as CH, SH, TH, ST, TR, CR and PL when the letters allow them. These are useful starting points, not rules: a word can separate those letters or use a different pattern entirely.

With T, R, A, C and E, testing TR at the beginning suggests TRACE. Testing CR suggests CRATE. Both use the same tiles. This is why finding a real anagram does not guarantee that you have found the game’s selected word.

3. Try a familiar ending

When a beginning does not appear, build from the other end. Endings such as -ER, -ED or -LY can help when the necessary tiles are present. An E at the end is another pattern to try. Then check whether the remaining letters make a familiar word.

Do not force a suffix just because it is available. Each candidate still needs to be a word, and it must use exactly the tiles in front of you.

4. Change the arrangement when you get stuck

Reading the same order repeatedly can make it harder to see alternatives. Try a different first letter, say a possible word quietly, or place the vowels in new slots. On the game board, use Clear to start arranging again before submission.

Tapping a filled answer slot removes that letter and the letters after it, so you can revise an ending without rebuilding a useful beginning. The timer keeps running while you rearrange.

5. Count the letters before you submit

A candidate can sound right while using the wrong letters. Repeated tiles are separate copies. If your tiles contain two Es, your answer must use two Es; one E cannot stand in for both.

  • Is the answer exactly five letters long?
  • Have you used each tile once, including repeated letters?
  • Have you added a letter that was not in the scramble?
  • Are you ready to commit your one daily submission?

What happens after your answer?

The result shows whether you found the selected word and records the outcome on your device. Playing is free without an account. Choose a public display name if you want to save your result to rankings. No sign-in is required. A wrong answer ends today’s scramble too, so taking a moment to review can be more useful than rushing.

A new daily challenge arrives at midnight in your device’s time zone. Once you have finished, you can try the daily five-letter word puzzle or daily trivia question.
